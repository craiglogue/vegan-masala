## Ingredients
- 2 tbsp oil
- 1 onion, finely chopped
- 3 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 tbsp grated ginger
- 1 tsp ground cumin
- 1 tsp ground coriander
- ½ tsp turmeric
- 1 tsp chilli powder (or to taste)
- 2 tsp garam masala
- 1 can chopped tomatoes
- 2 cans chickpeas, drained and rinsed
- 1 tsp salt, or to taste
- 120 ml water, plus more if needed
- 1 tbsp lemon juice
- 2 tbsp fresh coriander, chopped

## Method
1. Heat 2 tablespoons oil in a wide pan over medium heat. Add 1 finely chopped onion and fry for 8 to 10 minutes, stirring regularly, until soft, golden and beginning to sweeten.
2. Add 3 minced garlic cloves and 1 tablespoon grated ginger. Cook for 30 to 45 seconds, just until the raw smell fades.
3. Stir in 1 teaspoon ground cumin, 1 teaspoon ground coriander, ½ teaspoon turmeric, 1 teaspoon chilli powder and 2 teaspoons garam masala. Fry for 15 to 20 seconds over medium heat, stirring constantly so the spices bloom without catching.
4. Add 1 can chopped tomatoes and cook for 8 to 10 minutes, stirring now and then, until the mixture thickens, darkens slightly and loses its raw tomato smell.
5. Add 2 cans drained chickpeas, 1 teaspoon salt and 120 ml water. Stir well, lightly crushing a few chickpeas against the side of the pan to help the sauce thicken.
6. Bring to a gentle simmer and cook for 10 to 12 minutes, until the chickpeas have taken on the masala and the sauce is thick enough to coat them rather than pool around them. Add a little more water if the pan looks too dry.
7. Stir in 1 tablespoon lemon juice and most of the 2 tablespoons chopped fresh coriander. Taste and adjust the salt or chilli if needed.
8. Finish with the remaining coriander and serve hot with basmati rice, roti or naan.

## Notes
- Do not rush the onion and tomato stages. Much of the depth in chana masala comes from cooking the base until it tastes rounded rather than sharp.
- If you prefer a softer, more coated texture, mash a small spoonful of the chickpeas into the sauce during the final simmer.
- This is the kind of curry that often tastes even better the next day, once the chickpeas have had more time to absorb the spices.
- Leftovers keep well in the fridge for up to 3 days and can be frozen for up to 1 month.
